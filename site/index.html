A team of coding agents, in one window.

Hangar.AI runs Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and OpenCode side by side in real terminals, with a task board and a memory they share. Pin an orchestrator. It runs the others.

Orchestration turns one Claude Code pane into a lead that plans and delegates instead of writing code. Hangar does the parts a prompt should not be trusted with: the worktrees, the limits, the checks and the merge.

  1. 1

    Give it an objective

    In a git repository, pin any Claude Code pane as the workspace's orchestrator and say what you want. It reads the code, asks you the product questions it cannot settle itself, all in one go, then writes a plan with validation criteria to the shared memory.

  2. 2

    It plans on the board

    The plan becomes tasks on the workspace board, each with a goal, acceptance criteria, the command that proves it, and the files it owns. Hangar never runs two tasks at once whose files overlap, and a task waits for the ones it depends on.

  3. 3

    Workers take the tasks

    Each task goes to a worker: a new Claude Code pane, on Sonnet by default, on a branch of its own in its own git worktree, with 20 ports set aside so their dev servers do not fight over one. Three run at once by default, and Hangar refuses a fourth.

  4. 4

    Every delivery is checked before it lands

    A worker that is done hands in its branch. Hangar merges it in a scratch worktree, runs your check command if you set one (pnpm test, cargo test…), and has a reviewer agent with a fresh context read the diff. Only then does it move your branch forward. A step that fails goes back to the worker with the reason, and one delivery runs at a time per repository.

You stay in charge

One box gathers what waits on you: an agent's question, a product decision the orchestrator escalated, a delivery ready to merge. Deliveries merge by themselves by default; turn that off and each one waits for your click.

Limits a prompt cannot talk its way past

Workers at once, attempts per task, overlapping files, permissions, the check command and the branch work lands on are set per workspace and enforced by Hangar, not asked of the model. The models are set there too: by default Opus plans, Sonnet works and Sonnet reviews.

Hangar never pushes

Merges are local fast-forwards, and the orchestrator and its workers are told not to push. Pushing, pull requests and releases stay your call.

Woken by events, not a loop

The orchestrator ends its turn while workers run. Hangar wakes it when a task moves, a worker waits or stops, or a delivery lands or fails.

Survives compaction

After a restart or a compacted context, the orchestrator gets the board, its plan and the latest events back, and carries on from there.

Follow and steer your agents from your phone.

Hangar Mobile shows what waits for you in every project, and lets you answer, approve and chat with your agents while Hangar keeps running on your PC. It comes with Hangar: no app store, no account with us, no server of ours in between.

Hangar Mobile running on sample projects.

Everything that waits for you

For you gathers, across every project, the agents asking for a permission or an answer, the questions an orchestrator handed back to you, and the deliveries ready to land.

Answer and approve

Read any pane's screen as it updates, answer an agent, press Esc or Enter, attach a photo or a file. A permission shows what it will do, the command or the file, with Allow and Deny.

A real chat with Claude

A Claude Code pane opens as a conversation: your messages, Claude's answers with their formatting, one line per action. Its questions come as large buttons, and your answer reaches the session on the PC as if you had typed it there. Codex, Gemini CLI and shells keep the terminal view.

Files and notifications

Agents can send you images and files, a screenshot or a report, which wait under Shared. Notifications reach the phone even with the app closed, sent by your PC itself.

A few days away

Since your last visit shows what happened while you were away, kept for 30 days. Hangar can keep the PC from going to sleep, and start when you log in, with your workspaces and Hangar Mobile.

One agent for every project

The project manager follows all your workspaces: ask it where they stand, or have it pass an instruction to a project's orchestrator, waking a sleeping workspace. It speaks up only for what waits for you, and never writes code. Open it from the top of the sidebar, or the phone's Manager tab.

Private, through Tailscale

Hangar serves Hangar Mobile itself, and you add it to the Home Screen of an iPhone or an Android phone. It listens on 127.0.0.1 only: Tailscale, a free app that links your own devices over a private network, is the only way in, over HTTPS and only from your devices. Each phone pairs with a one-time code, and you can revoke it from the PC.

Set it up once, in about a minute

  1. Install Tailscale on the PC and on the phone, signed in to the same account.
  2. In Hangar, under Settings → Mobile, turn Hangar Mobile on, then HTTPS access.
  3. Scan the QR code with the phone, and add Hangar to its Home Screen.

Everything else

Hangar.AI started as a way to tile agents in one window. Here is what it grew around them.

An interface built like an IDE

A navigator lists every workspace and what its agents are doing. An inspector shows the focused agent's session, context window, cost and plan usage. A command palette reaches every pane, workspace and command from CtrlShiftP, or ⌘⇧P on a Mac.

33 themes restyle the whole window, not only the terminals. Graphite is the default, where colour only marks an agent's state, a diff or the focus. Try a few on this page:

See where the tokens go

Hangar reads the transcripts Claude Code and Codex keep on your machine and adds up tokens, active time and an API-equivalent cost by project, agent and model, and maps the hours of the week your agents were busy.

The cost prices every token at the providers' public API rates. It measures the work done, not what a Pro or Max subscription charges you.

Agents can show you a 3D model

Over MCP, an agent opens a glTF, GLB, OBJ, STL, PLY, FBX or USDZ file in a panel beside its terminal, then renders snapshots to check its own work. Each workspace has its own preview, and a model reloads when its file changes.

A board the agents share

A kanban board per workspace that agents read, claim and comment on over MCP. Claims are atomic: two agents never win the same task.

A memory across workspaces

Notes every agent can search and write, wherever it runs: conventions, decisions, the trap someone already fell into.

MCP servers per workspace

Pick which servers Claude Code loads in each workspace. Every session starts its own copy of each stdio server, so fewer means less memory.

Idle workspaces go to sleep

By default, a workspace you have not touched for 10 minutes, and are not looking at, stops its agents to free memory. They resume their sessions when you come back.

Git worktrees and diffs

Create and remove worktrees from the app, and read what an agent changed before it lands.

Sessions come back

Reopen the window and every Claude Code and Codex pane resumes the conversation it was in, with no session ids to keep track of.

Agent states at a glance

Every pane says whether its agent is working, waiting for your answer, or done. Claude Code reports it exactly, through a small bridge.

Dictation

Hold a key and speak into the focused pane. It runs on your machine with whisper.cpp, or on Groq if you prefer. Off until you turn it on.

Broadcast

Send one instruction to every pane at once. Claude Code panes can hold it until their agent is free.

Up to 16 panes per workspace, any shell

PowerShell, cmd, Git Bash, WSL and Nushell on Windows; zsh, bash, fish, Nushell and pwsh elsewhere.

Questions

What does it cost?

Hangar.AI is free, under the MIT license, with no account and no paid tier. The agents cost what they already cost you: each one uses its own subscription or API key.

Orchestration runs several sessions at once: an orchestrator, three workers by default and a reviewer for each delivery. It uses a plan's limits faster than a single agent. The models and the number of workers are yours to set per workspace.

Does it work with my Claude Pro or Max plan?

Yes. Hangar starts the claude command you already installed, with the login it already has, whether that is a plan or an API key. It never asks for your credentials. The navigator shows your plan's five-hour and weekly usage as Claude Code reports it.

Which agents and systems does it support?

Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and OpenCode, plus any shell. The orchestrator and its workers need Claude Code. Hangar.AI runs on Windows (64-bit), macOS (Apple Silicon and Intel) and Linux (x86-64), and Hangar Mobile on an iPhone or an Android phone, from its Home Screen.

Why does Windows warn me about the installer?

The Windows installer is not code-signed yet, so SmartScreen shows a warning the first time you run it. Choose More info, then Run anyway. The installer is built by GitHub Actions from the public source, and installs for your user only.

The macOS build is signed and notarised, so it opens after macOS's usual first-launch confirmation. If a release ever ships unsigned, its notes say so: then open it from System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway (right-click, Open before macOS 15).

Does my code or data leave my machine?

Not through Hangar. There is no server and no telemetry. The board lives in your project, in .hangar/board.json, kept out of git status; the memory, settings and statistics stay in the app's data folder.

Hangar goes online for four things: checking GitHub for updates, downloading the dictation model from Hugging Face once if you turn local dictation on, Groq, only if you pick it for dictation or for the optional cleanup of a transcript, and Hangar Mobile's notifications, once you turn them on, through your phone's push service, encrypted for that phone alone. Your agents talk to their providers exactly as they do outside Hangar.

Hangar Mobile is off until you turn it on. It listens on 127.0.0.1 only, and only your own devices reach it, through Tailscale.

What does Hangar Mobile need?

Hangar open on your PC, which stays on, and Tailscale, a free app, on the PC and on the phone, signed in to the same account. There is no app store, no account with us and no server of ours in between: Hangar serves the app itself, and you add it to the phone's Home Screen. On an iPhone, notifications need iOS 16.4 or later.

From the phone you answer, approve and chat; merging a delivery is still done on the PC. The README walks through the set-up, which takes about a minute.

Will the orchestrator push to my repository?

No. Workers commit on branches of their own, and a delivery lands on yours as a local fast-forward. Hangar itself never pushes, opens a pull request, tags or deploys, and the orchestrator and its workers are told not to either.

Where do I ask a question or share a setup?

On the Hangar.AI Discord. Bugs and feature requests are best filed as GitHub issues, where they can be tracked.
